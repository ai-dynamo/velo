# W3 and W4 design rulings: zero-RTT stream setup and the urgent lane

Dated 2026-09-04 (evening). This records the design research for the two workstreams the W0 measurement moved to the front of the plan (`velo-response-plane-win-plan.md`, evening addendum). Three read-only investigators mapped the attach protocol, the worker's send path, and the gate and rig surface; an adversarial synthesis verified the load-bearing claims against the code and ruled where the reports disagreed. Line numbers are on `drain-credit-return` at 692ba9a. Nothing here was measured; the arm brief below says what each arm can and cannot show.

## What the measurement said

At 512 workers in 8 processes and concurrency 8192, the worker-observed `_anchor_attach` round trip is 420 to 524 ms mean before `generate()` starts, the frontend's ingest FIFOs hold about 146 ms, and every mocker process shows egress backpressure that vanishes at concurrency 2048. The seat is the per-connection egress queue on the worker (bounded channel plus the admission gate's pending queue), above the wire. mux18p avoids both terms by construction: no attach round trip, and a writer that drains an urgent lane before ordered data.

## W3: zero-RTT stream setup, with prompt cancel in the same PR

**Ruling: W3-A plus W3-B in one PR.** The protocol does not have to be invented. `AnchorAttachResponse::Ok` already carries the five fields the worker needs (`streaming_transport_key`, `heartbeat_interval_ms`, `routing_session_id`, `initial_credit`, `slot_byte_budget`, `control.rs:220-256`), and every step of the handler that produces them is synchronous in practice: the mux's `bind` body is a bounded channel, a `DrainSignal`, two map inserts and a spawn with no await (`messenger_mux/mod.rs:940-985`). The receive side already carries the claim semantics: `register_bind` keys on `(anchor_id, session_id)` with no peer, so the frontend can pre-bind before a worker is chosen; `open_slot` claims by `binds.remove`; and it emits no `CreditUpdate` at claim time because the window was advertised. Zero-RTT changes when a computation runs, not what it computes.

- Frontend: `AnchorManager::prebind_anchor(handle) -> Option<StreamOpenTicket>`, the attach handler body with the active message removed, called by the adapter at request registration. `None` when no mux is installed is the complete rollback. `StreamOpenTicket` is a new velo type with exactly the five fields, built only through `NegotiatedLimits::from_wire` so the two asymmetric zeros are decided in one place.
- Envelope: `#[serde(default)] open: Option<StreamOpenTicket>` on the adapter's `VeloResponseConnectionInfo`. `PROTOCOL_VERSION` stays 1: a bump would break an old worker outright, while an absent field degrades to today's attach because the struct has no `deny_unknown_fields`.
- Worker: `Velo::open_anchor_stream(handle, ticket)`, the tail of `attach_remote` with the round trip cut out.
- Compatibility without a probe: an old frontend sends no ticket, so the worker attaches as today. A new frontend with an old worker receives `_anchor_attach` anyway and the handler adopts the unclaimed pre-bind (no second bind, no second pump, `attachment = true`, the ticket's own session id, credit and budget in the reply). A key mismatch refuses loudly and releases the pre-bind. The co-located path releases an outstanding pre-bind before using the anchor's own channel, or every co-located request leaks a bind, a drain entry and a reader pump for the 60 s accept window.
- Exactly-once: today the token is the atomic `attachment` flip; under zero-RTT it is `binds.remove` in `open_slot`. Both adoption interleavings (adopt then stray OpenSlot; OpenSlot then late attach) are pinned by tests, because this is the hole most likely to survive review.
- Orphans: `PreBind::Drop` releases an unclaimed bind or closes a claimed slot on every anchor-death path, which already remove the registry entry; the 60 s accept task stays as the backstop for an anchor that lives but is never opened.
- W3-B: zero-RTT never learns `stream_cancel_handle`, so cancel would degrade from prompt to reactive (for a worker mid-prefill, the rest of the prefill). Restored in band: `DrainSignal`'s claim records the slot id, and `close_claimed_slot(peer, slot)` posts `CloseSlot{UnknownSlot}` through the existing reply path. No wire change.
- Credit is exact by construction: the pre-bound buffer depth, the ticket's credit and budget, and `IngressSlot::new`'s values are one `NegotiatedLimits` read twice (`messenger_mux/mod.rs:462-470, 1007-1009`). A test pins the identity so a later split cannot reproduce the 2C-against-C+1 bug.
- `lib/velo-ext` is untouched. `FrameTransport::bind` is exactly what the pre-bind duplicates synchronously; the new methods are inherent on the concrete mux, joining `advertised_limits`, `connect_negotiated`, `take_drain_signal` for the reason `anchor.rs:660-675` already states.
- What W3 does not remove: `connect_negotiated` still awaits the OpenSlot flush's admission on the worker's egress queue. W3 turns two admission waits, two wire transits and a handler into one one-way admission wait. Expect the `velo3` arm to recover materially less than the whole round trip; `velo34` tests the mechanism.
- Fallback: lazy bind at OpenSlot (mint the ticket, bind when the OpenSlot arrives by resolving the anchor's channel from the registry). Simpler lifecycle, but it inverts a layer and loses the accept-window meaning. Take it only if the adoption interleavings prove unfixable.
- Gate: `DYN_VELO_RESPONSE_ZERO_RTT_ATTACH=0|1`, default 0, parsed with a hard error. A worker with the gate on and a ticketless envelope fails naming a stale frontend; a worker with the gate off still honours a present ticket.

## W4: two PRs, two gates, two arms

**Ruling: split W4 into W4a (detached-watch OpenSlot ack) and W4b (control lane at the connection writer).** They recover different terms by different means, and one gate would make the arm unattributable.

**W4a** (first, small, no wire or transport change). `on_open_slot` (`peer_batcher/mod.rs:501-542`) acks only after `flush().await`, which awaits admission on the egress queue, and `connect_negotiated` awaits that ack. This wait sits on top of the measured attach round trip and is invisible to the attach histogram. Change: restructure on the `send_singleton` pattern (`records.rs:232-278`): cut any staged data batch, stage the OpenSlot alone, hand it off without awaiting, fence the slot, ack immediately, and report the admission through the coalesced control path so a failed admission is still exactly one epoch death per live slot. The fence is load-bearing, not defensive: `fire()` can hand a frame to a detached task, so per-target FIFO alone does not keep the first data record behind its own OpenSlot. Rider: `dispatch_singleton` consumes a `batch_seq` it never refunds on its two failure arms; refund it. Gate: `DYN_VELO_RESPONSE_ASYNC_OPEN_ACK=0|1`, default 0, a `MuxConfig` field, sender-side only, no cross-process agreement needed. Arm `velo4a`.

**W4b** (second). `SendClass::{Control, Data}` at the connection writer, zero velo-ext change: a velo-internal `PrioritySend: Transport` supertrait and `VeloBuilder::add_transport_prioritized` that keeps a typed side map next to the erased vector, since the erasure happens at velo's own `add_transport`, not at velo-ext's boundary, and the adapter holds the concrete `TcpTransport` one line before erasing it. The TCP connection gets a second shallow channel and a second admission gate; `run_coalescing_writer` takes lanes in strict priority order with a one-lane fast path; a share cap bounds starvation. Ordering: a per-class `batch_seq` in bit 0 of the reserved `flags` byte (zero wire bytes), because one overtaken batch would add about 4.29e9 to `velo_streaming_mux_batch_seq_gaps_total`. What rides Control: OpenSlot-only batches and, while W3 is off, the attach request. What does not: data, terminal pairs, credit returns. A socket-buffer knob ships in the same PR (default today's 2 MiB, floor 256 KiB) because the lane's ceiling is the socket: send backpressure on every mocker proves the pinned 2 MiB send buffer was saturated, and no writer-level priority overtakes bytes already in the kernel. Gates: `DYN_VELO_RESPONSE_CONTROL_LANE=0|1`, `DYN_VELO_RESPONSE_CONTROL_SHARE=1..=1024` (default 32), `DYN_VELO_RESPONSE_SOCKET_BUFFER` (bytes, default 2097152). Arms `velo4` and `velo4b` (the 256 KiB measurement arm, never a ship candidate). A new counter `velo_transport_control_lane_frames_total{transport}` is the behavioural proof the post-run assertion needs.

Rejected: a second connection per peer pair (lazy connect means the control connection may not exist when the first OpenSlot goes, so it can land after data and truncate a stream's head); a new `MessageType` variant (the enum is not `non_exhaustive`, so it breaks velo-ext); a priority queue inside `AdmissionGate` (the fast path bypasses the queue whenever the channel has room, so it would reorder only the overflow); a defaulted `Transport::send_urgent` on velo-ext (a coordinated release for one in-tree caller). Naming: not `URGENT` (already a flush-policy word in the batcher) and not `SEND_CLASS`.

## Arms, gates, and the wheel

Arms: `velo2` (W2 (a) and (d)), `velo3`, `velo4a`, `velo4`, `velo4b`, `velo34`, `velof` (all lowercase: `summarize.py` lowercases directory names for its fallback match). Every existing arm's case branch, including the defaults, explicitly unsets every gate, and the resolved gates are logged by `VeloNode::build`, written into `rig_run_meta.json`, and asserted after the run the way the ucx transit check works. `summarize.py`'s arm tuple is sorted by length descending, with a unit test that says so.

One wheel carries all workstreams for a matrix: a disposable integration branch recreated before every wheel build from `drain-credit-return` (the base every code PR stacks on until it merges), merging each workstream branch with `--no-ff` in order (w0-ingest-metrics, w2, w3, w4a, w4b) with messages `integrate <branch> @ <sha>` so `git log --merges` reconstructs provenance; conflicts are expected in `observability.rs` and the adapter's `velo_response.rs` and are resolved on the integration branch only; a non-textual conflict (two branches reinterpreting one field or label) stops the build and goes back to the PRs. `build-wheel.sh` records the merge summaries and a stable marker so a rep is traceable to PR commits; the rig records both the wheel's velo sha and the live checkout's sha and fails a rep when they differ.

## Risks carried into the arm briefs

- `velo3` alone recovers less than the round trip; `velo34` tests the mechanism. Say so before the run.
- Streaming concurrency rises by construction (about 3,400 requests move from waiting-for-first-token to open); the claim that velo0's 6.8 s E2E p99 survives rests on the egress admission FIFO pacing the stream body. The falsifier is E2E p99 in `velo3`; the corroborating counter is `velo_streaming_mux_live_slots` on the frontend. If it fails, the replacement is a per-peer semaphore on open egress slots, not putting the round trip back.
- More open slots per frontend peer multiply the per-batch `collect_grants` walk (`ingest-cost-ledger.md`), which may pull W1-B back up the order.
- W4b's ceiling is the socket; the split between the socket, the 256-deep channel and the frontend's reply leg is W0b's measurement and should land before or with the arm. W4b does not move the first data record; presenting `velo4` as a TTFT arm gives a disappointing number and the wrong conclusion.
- Three verbatim-copied arm case statements going from two gates to seven is the highest-probability defect in the change; a script should diff the arm bodies for token-set and export-set equality.
